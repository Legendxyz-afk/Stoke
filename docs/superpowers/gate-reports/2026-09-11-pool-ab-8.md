# 2026-09-11 — three one-flag A/Bs on `stoke serve` @8: spin NO, pin NO, threads YES (+5.8% wall at 10T)

Follow-up to `2026-09-11-clause2-e2e-8.md`, whose placement trace exposed two
headroom candidates (11.7% of chunks on E-cores; the dispatcher claiming ~40%
more chunks than any helper). Each was priced by ONE flag on the adapter, plus a
third arm — the thread count — because the serve default of 8 comes from an
N=1 sweep (`pool.h`, 2026-08-04) and `pool.h`'s own note says to re-measure it
when the kernels get faster.

Instrument: `tests/tooling/run_e2e_interleaved.py`, one server lifetime per
unit, arms alternating with the order flipping every pair, 45 s cool-down, the
quiesce gate on every unit (it refused two units — `steamwebhelper`, then
`TiWorker` — which were re-run after the load left). Variants are
`stoke-tuned@spin=N`, `@pin=MASK`, `@threads=N` (`bench/run_matrix.build_adapter`),
and **every arm is verified from its own record**: `trace.spin` (the trace
header prints the budget the pool read), `proc.affinity_mask`, `trace.threads`.
18 of 18 measured units verify; `completed` 8/8 on every one. Scenario
`s2-closed-8`, binary `build/win/stoke.exe` at `c616f26` plus the trace-header
change committed with this report. Bank (merged, tracked):
`bench/results/2026-09-11-ab-threads.json`; raw `bench/results/2026-09-11-ab/*.jsonl`.

## 1. What the two refuted candidates say

- **Spin.** With `STOKE_POOL_SPIN=20000` the dispatcher-over-helper chunk ratio
  fell from 1.20–1.29 to 1.06–1.14 — the helpers did claim more chunks — and
  the wall did not move. So the helpers' deficit is per-chunk **speed**
  (cache-cold input, migration), not wake latency. The "40% more chunks"
  headroom named on 09-11 is **not headroom**; the 08-17 pricing of the spin
  (+2% at +15% CPU) is confirmed as not worth shipping.
- **Pin.** Eight threads on six physical cores lose 8.6% against eight threads
  free to use the E-cores. The 11.7% E-share is real work, not a tax.

## 2. The thread count, and why 10 is the candidate

| 16 | 0 | — | — | — | — | — | — |

- **10 vs 8: +5.8% wall for +13% CPU-seconds, both pairs, arm spreads under
  0.6%.** The E-share rises 0.115 → 0.175 — two more threads spill onto the
  four E-cores — which is consistent with the pin result (the E-cores carry
  real work) but is not an attribution of the gain.
- **12 vs 10: +1.5% for +15% more CPU, n=1.** The second 12T unit (62.86 s)
  disagrees with itself: its warmup aggregate is 5.2% above its measured one,
  where every other unit in the sitting agrees within 1.4% ⇒ disturbed (it
  ran while the session was being backgrounded), not a second reading.
- ⇒ **Candidate for the grading run: 10 threads.** 12 is the fallback lever
  if 10 falls just short; 16 is unmeasured.
- ⚠ `pool.h`'s N=1 sweep read 12T at 9.5 vs 10.6 tok/s for 8T — moving the
  serve default requires re-checking N=1 does not regress. Not done here.

## 3. Sitting drift, for the record

The spin bank (17:35–17:57, quiesce reads 5–10%) has 8T walls of 65.9–67.2 s;
the threads bank (18:12–18:22, reads 3–6%) has 64.8–65.2 s: the box quietened
~2% through the sitting. Every verdict above is within-bank and interleaved,
so none of them is touched by it; no absolute from one bank is comparable to
another's.

## 4. Disposition

- Clause 2's llama half stays **0.980× @8, UNGRADED at 10 threads**. Arithmetic
  says 0.980 × 1.058 ≈ 1.037× — a prediction, not a measurement; the grading

- Neither refuted candidate changes shipping code. The thread default is not
  moved by this report.
