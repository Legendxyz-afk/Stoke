## 3. What the placement trace says about a healthy `stoke serve`

`STOKE_POOL_TRACE=10`, 8 threads, unpinned over 16 LPs (`ecores=0xf000`):

- **11.4–11.8% of all chunks run on the four E-cores** (LPs 12–15), in
  every unit, steadily. Four of eight threads are handed an E-core as their
  ideal processor (`ideal = 2 10 12 13 14 15 1 3`, one unit `2 8 10 12 13 14 15
  1`), but the E-share is not ideal-driven: the P-ideal helpers run ~14% of
  their chunks on E-cores too; only the dispatching thread (ideal 2) stays on
  P-cores (2–5% E).
- **The dispatching thread claims ~40% more chunks than any helper**
  (1.35–1.65 M vs 0.99–1.17 M): it starts the instant it dispatches, the
  helpers wake ~250 times per token. That gap is the wake latency,
  measured for the first time as work rather than inferred. It is the
  quantity `STOKE_POOL_SPIN` trades CPU for (pool.c: +2% at +15% CPU-seconds
  on 2026-08-17) — a re-measure under the trace would say what the spin buys
  per thread rather than in aggregate.
- 8 threads at ~6.7 cores busy (815 CPU-s over ~121 s of traced time).

Whether the 11.7% E-share costs anything is **not measured**: pinning to
`0xFFF` stacks 8 threads on 6 physical cores and was priced at ~1.7× worse at
N=16 — at 8 threads it is an open A/B, one flag on the adapter.
