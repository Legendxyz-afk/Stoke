# 2026-09-14 — B7's RSS probe: the climb is first-touch, and the instrument that can say so

## STATUS: the 09-06 "RSS climbing, no verdict" is resolved by reading and by measurement — **the climb is first-touch of the calloc'd KV arena at page granularity, bounded by the arena; mapped memory does not grow.** The soak driver's RSS instrument was incapable of a verdict and is replaced; two gaps in the soak's grading ("no stall" had no instrument) are closed in the same file. **Results: v1 CLIMBING by its own rule (§4, and why that rule was wrong at page granularity); v3 FLAT by the corrected, pre-registered rule (§5), arena saturated at exactly its size with the non-arena resident set constant to the MiB across 500 generations.**

Nothing here is a 24 h soak result. B7's soak is still owed, after the freeze.

---

## 1. The question, and why it had no answer

`d2d4521` (the soak driver, shaken down 09-06) found RSS climbing 2568 → 2761
MB in six minutes on the plain build and 2717 → 2975 MB across the first two
chunks of the 1 h probe, which then died at 2 of 12 chunks. Its instrument was
`VmRSS` alone, and `VmRSS` is anonymous memory **plus** file-backed pages —
two quantities that both grow benignly here for a while:

- the model is `mmap`'d (`gguf.c:310`, `MAP_PRIVATE`, `PROT_READ`), so its
  2381 MiB enter RSS as they are first read;
- the KV arena is `xcalloc`'d (`kv.c:108-109`), so its pages enter RSS as they
  are first **written**.

A leak also grows RSS and never stops. A total cannot separate them; that is
the whole reason the 09-06 run could not conclude.

## 2. What reading the code predicts

`blockpool.c:41-63`: the free list is a **FIFO** — `fl_pop_head` allocates from
the head, `fl_push_tail` frees to the tail. So every block in the pool is
popped once before any block is popped twice.

`kv.c:52-53,106-107`: the arena is laid out `[n_layer][capacity][16 tokens]
[n_head_kv]` vectors of `head_dim × 4` bytes. One **token row** of one block in
one layer is `n_head_kv × head_dim × 4 = 8 × 128 × 4 = 4096 B — exactly one
page**. So first-touch is per *(block, position)*, not per block: a sequence of
14 tokens in a block touches 14 of its 16 rows in every layer, and the last
two rows stay untouched until some later sequence writes them.

One block at this model's dims: `2 × 36 × 16 × 8 × 128 × 4 = 4,718,592 B =
4.5 MiB` (K and V). At the soak's `--ctx 1024 --seqs 4` the pool is 256
blocks = **1152 MiB**; at the probe's `--ctx 256 --seqs 4` it is 64 blocks =
**288 MiB**.

**The 09-06 slope has a fingerprint.** Plain build: +270 MB over 56
generations = 4.8 MB/gen. ASan build: +258 MB over 57 = 4.5 MB/gen. One block
is 4.5 MiB. Under the fuzz's 14–28-token requests (`serve_fuzz.c:149-154`,
`max_tok ≤ 3`) each generation pops one or two fresh blocks. That is H1: the
climb is the FIFO walking a calloc'd arena, bounded by the arena.

H2, the same slope from a leak, is not refuted by reading — a KV block never
returned would look identical until the pool ran out. The 09-06 run reached
103 generations on a 256-block pool, so it could not have run out. The two
hypotheses separate only on a run long enough to pass the arena's size.

## 3. The instrument

`tests/tooling/run_m7_soak.sh` now samples, every 5 s for the server's whole
life and per chunk in the bank:

| column | source | what it separates |
|---|---|---|
| `anon_mb` | `RssAnon` | touched anonymous memory |
| `file_mb` | `RssFile` | the model paging in |
| `data_mb` | `VmData` | **mapped** private memory — a leak of any kind, brk or mmap, must grow it; first-touch cannot |
| `arena_mb` | `/proc/pid/pagemap` over the arena's address range | how much of the arena itself has been written |

and `--rss-probe` mode runs the soak's own server and traffic at the 64-block
pool so saturation is reachable inside a run, with a pre-registered reading
in the script header (VOID / CLIMBING / FLAT) whose thresholds are the arena
size and one block, both computed from the model header before the run.

**The arena reader took three versions on the same day, each caught by its
own output:**

1. *v1* had no arena reader; it defined "walked" as `gens ≥ n_blocks` (every
   generation pops ≥ 1 block, so after 64 pops all 64 blocks have been popped).
   True, and the wrong granularity — a popped block is not a fully-written
   block (§2).
2. *v2* matched the arena's VMA in `smaps` by size. The kernel merges adjacent
   anonymous mappings with equal flags: at t=0 the two arenas showed as **one**
   VMA of 2 × (147456 + 4) KiB (the first reader, which looked for two,
   matched nothing and the pre-flight aborted — as designed); then a later
   mapping (~37 MiB of VmData in the first minute) merged into the same VMA
   and it no longer had the arena's size. Every chunk read `arena 0/288`
   while anon climbed. Killed at chunk 5.
3. *v3* locates the arena once at t=0 (by size, asserted to add up) and keeps
   its **address range**; residency is then read per page from `pagemap`
   (bit 63 present, bit 62 swapped — readable unprivileged), which no later
   mapping can disturb.

What the same numbers say about the rule:

- **VmData is flat** across the same halves (−3 MiB) and across the whole run
  after the first minute (372 → 390 → 390). No mapping grew. A leak — brk or
  mmap, touched or not — grows VmData.
- **RssAnon converged on the number predicted from parts**: baseline 26 +
  mapped growth 17 (VmData 372 → 389 after the first generations) + arena 288
  = **331**; observed **333**, with the last four chunks at +2, 0, +1, +1.
- The slope during the pop-walk: (215 − 75) / (40 − 10) gens = **4.67
  MiB/gen** against one block = 4.5 MiB. After the walk the slope decays as
  the untouched rows are the only pages left to touch.

So v1's "CLIMBING" is the arena's last rows filling in at page granularity,
which its walk-end definition did not model — a derivation error in the
pre-registered rule, corrected in v2/v3 by reading the arena's residency
rather than inferring it. The rule is not re-fitted: v1's verdict is kept as
written and the corrected rule was frozen before the v3 run.

## 6. What this means for B7

- **The RSS reading the soak needs is `VmData` for the leak class and the
  arena's own residency for the plateau**, both now banked per chunk. `VmRSS`
  alone cannot grade a soak and should not appear alone in one again.
- At the soak's 1152 MiB pool the page-level saturation takes hours under the
  fuzz's short requests (the 64-block pool took ~16 min at ~15 gens/min; the
  256-block pool has 4× the rows, and the fuzz's requests touch the same
  fraction of each). The 24 h series will show a long decaying climb of
  RssAnon **that is not evidence of anything** as long as VmData is flat and
  `arena_mb` stays under 1152. RSS stays REPORTED, not gated, as the driver
  already said; what is now gateable is `VmData`.
- Two grading gaps closed in the driver: a **canary** after every chunk (one
  non-streaming chat, 60 s deadline, 200 required) and a **zero-exchange chunk
  check** — the gate says "no stall" and nothing had ever tested for one:
  `serve_fuzz` discards replies and counts any bytes read as an exchange.
- The mapped growth of 17–37 MiB in the first minute of traffic (VmData 341 →
  372–378) is the process's first-traffic allocations (per-sequence scratch,
  thread arenas, HTTP buffers); it is flat thereafter in both runs. Not chased.
