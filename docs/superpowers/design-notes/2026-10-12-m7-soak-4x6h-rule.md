# M7 gate [3] — the soak as four 6 h runs, pre-registered

*2026-10-12, Opus 5.5. Kabir decided the shape the same day. Written and
committed BEFORE the first run. Code: `tests/tooling/soak_rss_rule.sh` (the
grader; its threshold is now a rate), `tests/tooling/test_soak_rss_rule.sh`
(27 cases), `tests/tooling/run_m7_soak.sh` (the 6 h mode). `src/` is
untouched, and the freeze stands (the freeze memo lets `tests/tooling/`
move). This note amends `2026-09-23-m7-soak-rss-rule.md`; everything there not
changed here still holds.*

## 1. The decision

- **Kabir, 2026-10-12:** gate [3] runs as **four separate 6 h runs**, 24 h of
  traffic in all. This PC cannot be given a continuous day. He asked "can we
  do this in intervals?", then "cant we split it into 6hrs 3 chunks?", and
  chose four runs over three and over a rented cloud VM running the
  continuous 24 h.
- **What he was told first:** a restart loses the single-lifetime evidence
  (the rig is built around one server per run); light use of the PC during a
  24 h run would have been fine; pausing the traffic across days keeps one
  lifetime but needs the PC on for three days or more.
- **Why it is acceptable now:** v0.1 is the Lorren desktop app (Kabir, the
  same day). The app starts a fresh engine every time it opens or switches
  model, so an engine's lifetime is bounded by an app session. The failures a
  user would meet (a crash, a memory error, a leak the leak checker sees at
  exit, a stall) are covered as before.

## 2. What changes

| | the 24 h run (2026-09-23) | the four 6 h runs (this note) |
|---|---|---|
| server lifetimes | one, 24 × 60-min chunks | four, 6 × 60-min chunks each |
| traffic | 24 h | 24 h (4 × 6 h) |
| seeds | 20260906 | 20261012, 20261018, 20261024, 20261030 (run k = 20261012 + 6(k − 1)); **amended 2026-10-13 before run 2, §8** — registered first as 20261012–15 |
| ASan quarantine | 64 MiB | 16 MiB |
| RSS window | the final 6 h | the final 3 h of each run |
| RSS threshold | one KV block, 4.50 MiB | half a block, 2.25 MiB: the same rate, one block per 6 h of window (~0.75 MiB/h) |
| pass | `=== M7 soak: OK (…, RSS FLAT) ===` | that line from all four runs |

**Unchanged:** the rig (one server per run that ends itself by
`--max-seconds`, a chunked driver, the server and the dmesg witness at
`oom_score_adj -1000`, the witness banked to `/mnt/m`); the floor of ten
completed generations from the server's own log; the canary (HTTP 200 within
60 s after every chunk); the sanitizer check; LeakSanitizer at the server's
exit; the per-chunk traffic-mix bank; the shakedown before each run; the
frozen binary and its hash line.

## 3. What it proves, and what it does not

- **Each run proves:** no crash, no sanitizer report, no leak LeakSanitizer
  can see at exit, no stall, and memory flat over the run's last 3 h at
  ~0.75 MiB/h resolution. Across the four: 24 h of randomized hostile
  traffic.
- **It does not prove** that one engine process stays healthy for a whole
  day. Each run starts fresh, so nothing here watches a process past 6 h.
  Growth that only begins after hour 6 is outside it. A bounded fill that
  takes longer than 3 h to finish fails, conservatively: "not shown bounded"
  is not "bounded".
- **Owed:** a continuous 24 h run, on a rented cloud VM if this PC still
  cannot host one, before any server-style release (the headless gateway in
  the universal-server spec). Until then, README and BENCHMARKS say "four 6 h
  runs", never "24 h".

## 4. The constants

- **Window, 3 h:** the second half of a 6 h run. The fill must end by hour 3.
- **Quarantine, 16 MiB:** it fills in ~50 min at the baseline's ~19 MiB/h,
  and before hour 3 at down to ~27% of that rate. At 64 MiB the fill would
  take ~3.5 h and run into the window. The quarantine is still only the
  likeliest cause of the baseline's climb, unproven (09-23 note, §2). A freed
  chunk stays poisoned for most of an hour of this server's frees, so a
  use-after-free has to reach back further than that to escape.
- **Threshold, a rate:** the grader now computes the threshold as one block ×
  window / 21600. The 24 h run's 6 h window grades exactly as before, and its
  17 cases are unchanged.
- **Steps:** a permanent +21 MiB step (the baseline had one at ~20 min) fails
  a run when it lands in the final 3 h, as it would have failed the 24 h in
  its final 6 h. The four runs grade 12 h of window in all, against the 24 h
  run's 6 h, so a late step is likelier to land in one: a real one is likelier
  caught, and a benign one costs a re-run.
- **Still diagnostic:** if a run still climbs at ~18 MiB/h through its last
  3 h with a 16 MiB quarantine, the quarantine was not the cause.

## 5. Direction check (Kabir decided; Opus records)

- **Harder or easier?** Easier on one axis: the single-lifetime claim is gone,
  and it is owed. Stricter on another: a fill must finish by hour 3, not hour
  18. The leak-rate threshold is the same.
- **Fitted to a result?** No. No run of this shape exists. The constants
  follow from the 09-23 numbers and from the window.
- **The cost of being wrong.** A false CLIMBING costs a 6 h re-run. A false
  FLAT would pass a slow growth into Phase 1's close, at the same rate as
  before.

## 6. How it was verified (2026-10-12, no server started)

- **RED first.** Against the unchanged grader, the ten new 6 h cases failed
  exactly where the scaling matters: `nonarena_leak6` and `mapped_leak6`, each
  +2.7 MiB, read FLAT under the unscaled 4.50 MiB.
- **GREEN.** 27/27 under Git Bash, 27/27 under WSL dash with gawk, and 27/27
  under WSL dash with awk resolved to mawk 1.3.4.
- **Mutants.** Each is a one-line change, and each turned the test red:

  | mutant | caught by |
  |---|---|
  | threshold left at one block for every window | `nonarena_leak6`, `mapped_leak6` |
  | threshold scaled per 24 h instead of per 6 h | `slow`, `slow6` |
  | the window argument ignored | `fill1`, `step2` |

- **Wiring.** The verdict block and the quarantine lines were extracted
  verbatim from `run_m7_soak.sh` and run under WSL dash with stubs, 8/8:
  - 6 h flat: RSS FLAT;
  - 6 h at +1 MiB/h: RSS NOT FLAT, and the run fails;
  - 24 h flat: RSS FLAT, labelled "final 6 h";
  - a 1 h soak and the shakedown: not graded;
  - quarantine 16 MiB for a 6 h soak, 64 MiB for 24 h and for the shakedown.

## 7. Running it

- **Each run:** `build/wslmatrix/launch_soak6h.sh <k>` as root, k = 1..4.
  The shakedown first, and the 6 h only on its OK; ~6 h 15 min of wall time.
  For those hours: ~4 GB of host RAM, no shutdown, no sleep, no game. Light
  use is fine.
- **Verdict:** the `=== M7 soak:` line in `build/m7_soak6h_run<k>.out`; the
  memory reading is on the `RSS rule (gate [3] 4 x 6 h scheme, seed …, final
  3 h):` line. The run banks to
  `docs/superpowers/gate-reports/<date>-<HHMM>-m7-soak.*`.
- **A failed run is not re-rolled with another seed.** The cause is found
  first, and a BUG fix-cycle restarts gate [3] from zero (freeze memo). A VOID
  run, where the reading does not exist, is re-run with the same seed.
- The runs may fall on different days; the PC is free between them.
- **Afterwards:** Hyper-V-Worker-Admin event 18508 says how the VM ended.

## 8. Amendment, 2026-10-13: the seeds of runs 2–4 (before run 2)

*Opus 5.5, during run 1 (started 2026-10-13 09:12 IST, stamp
`2026-10-13-0342-m7-soak`). Kabir approved it the same morning. Run 1 is
untouched: its seed stays 20261012.*

- **The finding.** `run_m7_soak.sh` gives chunk i the seed `SEED + i`
  (line 508; run 1's first chunk printed `seed 20261013`). With the run seeds
  registered as consecutive numbers, 20261011 + k, the four runs share five of
  their six chunk seeds pairwise: run 2's chunks are run 1's chunks shifted by
  one. All 24 chunks together held only **9 distinct traffics**
  (20261013–20261021), where the single 24 h run they stand in for would have
  held 24.
- **The amendment.** Run k uses seed **20261012 + 6(k − 1)**: 20261012,
  20261018, 20261024, 20261030. The chunk seeds become 20261013–20261036 with
  no repeats, which is exactly the chunk traffic of one 24 h run at seed
  20261012. The four runs now replay the 24 h run's traffic in four
  lifetimes, which is the closest stand-in this scheme can be.
- **Nothing else changes:** the grader, the window, the threshold, the
  quarantine, the pass line, and §7's rules (a failed run is not re-rolled
  with another seed; a VOID run is re-run with ITS seed, the amended one for
  runs 2–4).
- **Why it is legitimate now:** runs 2–4 have not started. The amendment is
  committed before run 2, with the reason, and it changes no verdict already
  read.
- **Launcher:** `build/wslmatrix/launch_soak6h.sh` (git-ignored) computes
  `seed=$((20261012 + 6 * (k - 1)))`, edited after run 1's chain had `exec`'d
  into `run_m7_soak.sh`, so the running run never read the change.
  `run_m7_soak.sh` itself was not edited.

## 9. Amendment, 2026-10-13: the guest clock (before run 2)

*Opus 5.5, during run 1. Kabir chose this handling the same morning: run 1
counts, with its real traffic recorded; runs 2–4 start only behind a clock
check.*

- **The finding.** The WSL guest's monotonic clock runs fast. Its
  clocksource is raw `tsc`, and the kernel detected the TSC at 2611.197 MHz
  at boot. Measured three ways: the guest's `/proc/uptime` read 8,041 s when
  the host said the VM had started 7,403 s earlier (ratio 1.086); the guard
  below measured 1.0901 ± 0.0018 against the host clock over 220 s; and run
  1's first two "3600 s" chunks took 3,346 s and 3,326 s of real time,
  canary included. The guest's WALL clock is synced from the host and stayed
  right, which is why every timestamp looked correct.
- **What it touches.** Anything timed on the guest's monotonic clock:
  `serve_fuzz`'s chunk length, the server's `--max-seconds`, the canary's
  seconds, any duration or rate measured inside WSL. It does NOT touch the
  RSS rule: its `t_s` comes from the wall clock (checked against the host to
  the second at 1 h 54 min), so "the final 3 h" are real hours.
- **Run 1 counts** (Kabir). Its record states its real traffic, about 5.5 h
  (six chunks of ~3,315 s of fuzzing plus canaries), not 6 h. Its sanitizer,
  LeakSanitizer and RSS evidence is unaffected. The server's budget
  (`TOTAL_SECS + 120`) runs on the same fast clock as the chunks, so the
  drift cancels; what remains is the per-chunk overhead (~34 s for chunk 1,
  ~12 s after), which leaves ~26 s after chunk 6's canary. Thin, but positive.
- **The guard,** `tests/tooling/guest_clock_check.sh`: it reads the host
  clock twice through WSL interop, 240 s apart, each reading bracketed by two
  guest `/proc/uptime` readings, and compares the two intervals. It refuses
  (exit 2) when they differ by more than 1%, or when its own uncertainty
  exceeds 0.5%, so a measurement that cannot judge never reads as a pass. Its
  verdict logic has a `--judge` mode; ten synthetic cases passed (1.0% and
  under pass; 8.6% fast, 9% slow, a noisy measurement, a short interval and
  broken or missing readings all refuse). **Run red first on the drifting VM:
  `guest clock: REFUSED -- ... ratio 1.0901 (+/- 0.0018)`, exit 2.**
- **Where it runs:** `build/wslmatrix/launch_soak6h.sh` calls it before the
  shakedown; a refusal starts nothing. Its printed `guest clock:` line is the
  verdict.
- **The fix, after run 1 ends and before run 2:** add
  `kernelCommandLine = clocksource=hyperv_clocksource_tsc_page` to
  `.wslconfig` (that clocksource is available in this guest, and it takes
  its scale from the hypervisor instead of the boot-time calibration),
  restart the VM, and run the guard: it must print `guest clock: OK` before
  any run 2. If it does not, a further amendment comes before any run.
- **Nothing else changes:** the seeds (§8), the grader, the window, the
  threshold and the pass line.
